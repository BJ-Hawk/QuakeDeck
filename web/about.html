About the project

A clearer view when Japan moves.

QuakeDeck began with a simple question: could an earthquake monitor feel immediate and useful without being visually overwhelming?

What brought it on

Japan’s earthquake information is detailed, fast-moving, and consequential. QuakeDeck is a personal Android project built to make that information easier to follow: a map-first view, clear intensity colouring, readable event history, and a calmer interface around the moments that matter.

What inspired it

The project takes its inspiration from Japan’s public earthquake-information ecosystem: the work of the Japan Meteorological Agency, the communities and services that make public seismic information easier to access, and the disciplined clarity of serious monitoring tools. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by any of them.

What exists now

The prototype combines recent P2PQuake/JMA reports, observed Shindo mapping, tsunami information, historical browsing, station-level detail, and deterministic sandbox testing. It is deliberately map-first: detail appears as you zoom, while the overview stays legible.

Where it is heading

The next large milestone is a carefully integrated DM-D.S.S live feed: browser-based OAuth, reliable WebSocket reception, successive EEW updates, cancellations, and live observations. The DM-D.S.S probe remains available for controlled entitlement testing while that work is in progress.

Always use official guidance. QuakeDeck is an independent development project, not an official warning service. Follow JMA alerts and the instructions of local authorities during an emergency.
