About the project

A clearer view when Japan moves.

QuakeDeck began with a simple question: could an earthquake monitor feel immediate and useful without being visually overwhelming?

Who we are

We are an independent group of Android builders who care deeply about Japan: its places, its people, and the public systems that help people make sense of a rapidly changing situation. QuakeDeck is our attempt to make that respect visible in a tool that is calm, clear, and genuinely pleasant to use.

What brought it on

Japan’s earthquake information is detailed, fast-moving, and consequential. We wanted an English-first Android app that could present it with the same seriousness as the underlying information—without forcing people through a cluttered interface, a static regional summary, or a desktop-only workflow. We could not find an Android app in English that brought together a deep Japan map, live context, and a careful visual treatment of observed Shindo in the way we wanted, so we began building it.

What inspired it

The project takes its inspiration from Japan’s public earthquake-information ecosystem: the work of the Japan Meteorological Agency, the communities and services that make public seismic information easier to access, and the disciplined clarity of serious monitoring tools. We also acknowledge JQuake Desktop as an important reference point that helped make the gap—and the possibility of an English-first Android companion—more obvious. QuakeDeck is not affiliated with or endorsed by JMA, JQuake, or any data provider.

What exists now

The prototype combines recent P2PQuake/JMA reports, observed Shindo mapping, tsunami information, historical browsing, station-level detail, and deterministic sandbox testing. It is deliberately map-first: detail appears as you zoom, while the overview stays legible.

Municipality-level visual Shindo

At its deepest map level, QuakeDeck colours individual municipalities and designated-city wards from observed station reports. That lets a report show where shaking was actually observed within a prefecture, rather than treating the entire prefecture as one colour. We have not found this depth of visual Shindo reporting in another English-language Android earthquake app, and it is one of the details we are proudest of.

Where it is heading

The next large milestone is a carefully integrated DM-D.S.S live feed: browser-based OAuth, reliable WebSocket reception, successive EEW updates, cancellations, and live observations. The DM-D.S.S probe remains available for controlled entitlement testing while that work is in progress.

Always use official guidance. QuakeDeck is an independent development project, not an official warning service. Follow JMA alerts and the instructions of local authorities during an emergency.
